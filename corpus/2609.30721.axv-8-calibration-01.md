# Reading memo — 2609.30721v1 — When 10,000 Windows Are Not 10,000 Tests: Auditing Statistical Confidence in Sliding-Window Time-Series Classification

**Paper:** When 10,000 Windows Are Not 10,000 Tests: Auditing Statistical Confidence in Sliding-Window Time-Series Classification - Xinze Shi et al., [arXiv:2609.30721](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30721v1)

**Claim.** At 75% sliding-window overlap the count of test rows is a bad proxy for the count of independent evidence: i.i.d. observed-record inference has 16.9% Type-I error where 5% is nominal, session-centred Bartlett-HAC brings that to 7.2% without eliminating it, and on the authors' own frozen WISDM and HARTH predictions a 3.93×–3.97× growth in test rows buys only a 1.75×–1.94× growth in variance-equivalent information. ([arXiv:2609.30721](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30721))

**What advanced.** The baseline is the practice the paper is arguing with, not a weaker system: pooled i.i.d. normal inference on the raw test windows, and for the new-subject target the two target-misaligned alternatives (window-level i.i.d. and within-session HAC).

- **Calibration, null, Figure 3(a) and §V-D.** Type-I error at 0/50/75% overlap: i.i.d. **5.4 / 8.5 / 16.9%** historically and **5.2 / 8.7 / 16.9%** on the authors' independently re-seeded run; formal session-centred Bartlett-HAC **5.9 / 5.5 / 7.15%** historically and **5.5 / 6.4 / 7.9%** re-seeded. The re-seed is the paper's own and it moved the headline HAC figure by 0.75 pp.
- **Target misalignment, Figure 3(b).** New-subject target under subject/session heterogeneity at 75% overlap: i.i.d. **72.4%**, within-session HAC **66.2%**, equal-subject paired inference **5.5%**. The 66.9 pp gap is the paper's real result; the 16.9-vs-7.2 gap is the popular one.
- **Real data, Table IV and Figure 5.** Fixed-record paired Accuracy-difference 95% CI widths at 75% overlap, in percentage points, i.i.d. → HAC: WISDM 5 s **0.510 → 0.668** (ratio 1.31), WISDM 10 s **0.743 → 0.906** (1.22), HARTH 10 s **0.520 → 0.862** (1.66). Absolute widths, not percentages of a percentage.
- **Information growth, Figure 4 and Table IV.** Nominal window growth 3.93×–3.97×; variance-equivalent information growth **1.94 / 1.87 / 1.75** for WISDM 5 s, WISDM 10 s, HARTH 10 s. The shortfall is the paper's actual contribution and it is much larger than the 9.7 pp headline gap suggests.
- **Metric choice flips the conclusion, Table V and Table VI.** On HARTH at 75% overlap the paired Accuracy-difference interval includes zero for all three targets (**+0.07 pp** fixed, CI [−0.36, +0.50]; **+0.07 pp** subject, CI [−4.55, +4.69]) while Macro-F1 favours MiniROCKET by **13.21 pp** (CI [−16.03, −10.39]). Two classes, stair ascent/descent, at 1.08% and 0.59% of windows, contribute 7.34 pp of that 13.21.
- **Compute and protocol.** No GPU. 1,200 real trajectories, two classifier families (Random Forest on statistical+spectral features; MiniROCKET + validation-selected ridge), subject-level five-fold out-of-fold prediction, training windows at 50% overlap and test windows at 0/50/75%, and 1,000–2,000 Monte Carlo sets per condition. The full artifact is released (`xinze8806-ship-it/sliding-window-confidence-audit`, v1.0.0) including the frozen prediction files and the estimation code, which is rare and is what made the AXV re-run possible.

**Mechanism.** Overlapping windows share raw samples, so the window-level paired correctness indicator is a positively autocorrelated sequence whose correlation range is mechanically `window length / stride`; pooling it as i.i.d. understates the variance of its mean, and the understatement grows with the number of overlapping neighbours. Correcting for that requires not only a dependence-robust variance but one whose *unit* matches the population the claim is about, which is why a dependence correction that is nearly calibrated for the fixed-record target (7.2%) is still wildly miscalibrated for a new-subject target (66.2%). — evidence: **partially-evidenced**. The simulation half is close to `demonstrated`: marginal accuracies are pinned at 0.80/0.80, overlap is the manipulated variable, and the paper's own sensitivity grid varies bandwidth, block method, subject count, session heterogeneity and cross-model correlation separately. But the real-data half is observational on two datasets with one sensor each, and the paper itself reports that the sub-unity 0%-overlap ratios decompose into a centering factor times a dependence factor, which is a *different* mechanism operating below the overlap level. The information-growth figures are not isolated from label persistence, class balance and session composition, and the paper says so.

**Alternative routes.**

1. **Evaluate on disjoint windows and keep plain i.i.d. — no estimator at all.** Mechanism: if you never overlap, the only dependence left is ordinary serial correlation between adjacent windows, and the paper's own numbers say that dependence factor is small. Pros: zero machinery, no bandwidth to choose or defend, no target to declare, nothing to go stale when the data changes, and a shorter paper; the i.i.d. interval is nearly calibrated at 0% overlap (5.53% in AXV's 3-seed pool, 5.2–5.4% reported). Cons: you discard ~75% of the rows you already paid to generate, and it is still an approximation rather than a correction, because §V-B reports that adjacent *non*-overlapping windows remain temporally dependent, which is why the 0%-overlap HAC/i.i.d. ratios are 0.92, 0.84 and 1.10 rather than exactly 1.0; and it caps temporal resolution, which matters if the deployment claim is about short events. **Cost to test:** re-run the same frozen predictions on the 0%-overlap grid, which the authors already ship. **Would-it-win: yes, for the fixed-record target, on the paper's own numbers.** AXV computed the trade directly: the i.i.d. interval at 0% overlap is 1.20× (HARTH), 1.51× (WISDM 5 s) and 1.64× (WISDM 10 s) *wider* than the paper's headline 75%-overlap HAC interval, so you buy near-nominal calibration for a 20–64% wider interval and no estimator at all. For a company that runs a handful of runs per claim, that is a good trade and it is the route the paper does not take.

2. **Adopt the already-published clustered paired machinery instead of a bespoke session-centred HAC.** Mechanism: Hong et al. 2026 (arXiv:2606.03656) give a cluster sandwich for paired *nonlinear* confusion-matrix metrics, and Anglin 2026 (arXiv:2606.26422) give intervals for nested classifier data with effective sample size and design degrees of freedom. Either can be pointed at the session or the subject directly, which is the paper's own target-alignment argument. Pros: no new estimator to justify; handles Macro-F1, which is the metric the paper shows flips conclusions, and which the bespoke HAC handles only via an influence-function construction; already includes power analysis, which this paper needs and does not report for the real data; shorter method section. Cons: the paper's Table I is explicit that a sliding-window paired implementation is *not shown* for either, so adopting one is itself an unverified extrapolation; and the target that matters most here, HARTH's 22 subjects, is exactly the small-cluster regime Cameron & Miller 2015 warn about, where the sandwich's own approximation is weakest. **Why-not-chosen** (inference): the paper's stated contribution is the executable claim-to-analysis mapping, not a new estimator — §II says so in as many words, "our contribution is the executable claim-to-analysis mapping, not a new generic variance estimator" — so a paper built on someone else's estimator would be a tutorial. **Cost to test:** one session-clustered and one subject-clustered arm on the released prediction files. **Would-it-win: maybe** — plausibly yes on calibration, and it would delete most of §III-C.

3. **Session-level block bootstrap as the primary estimator instead of a Bartlett bandwidth.** Mechanism: resample contiguous blocks of sessions, which needs no bandwidth formula. Pros: it removes the one rule the paper admits was never independently validated — §IV-C: "The final historical HAC recalculation reused the earlier simulation seeds. Existing records do not establish independence between bandwidth selection and reported calibration." The paper already ships the session-aware block bootstrap and reports it agrees with the influence-function Macro-F1 interval to within rounding (§V-D), so the machinery exists and is validated. Cons: a block bootstrap needs a block length, so the tuning problem is renamed rather than solved; at 393–409 HARTH sessions with a mean length of ~27 windows a session-level block bootstrap degenerates toward the unblocked case, which is the regime where i.i.d. is worst; and it is roughly two orders of magnitude slower per replicate, which is presumably why the paper demotes it to a sensitivity check. **Why-not-chosen** (inference): the paper wanted a closed-form estimator for the primary path so the audit could be rerun on a laptop in seconds. **Cost to test:** one arm at 1,999 replicates on the released files. **Would-it-win: no** for the primary path, **yes** if the block length were pre-registered rather than tuned.

**Evidence quality.** Seeds: the real-data analysis is a single run on frozen out-of-fold predictions, and the paper says explicitly that the five OOF folds are "a descriptive training-set stability check, not five independent inference units." The Monte Carlo is properly seeded, and here the evidence is unusually self-critical: the authors re-ran the whole calibration with new seeds after review and reported that the headline HAC rate moved 7.15% → 7.9% and the WISDM 5 s length-stress moved 8.85% → 6.7%, a 2.12 combined-MCSE difference, while keeping both as separate outcomes and stating "Original rule-selection/calibration independence remains unverified." Ablations: strong on the estimator axis (ten methods in the frozen engine, three bandwidth variants, influence function vs block bootstrap for Macro-F1, per-session vs global bandwidth, four-level bandwidth sensitivity) and absent on the model axis, which is defensible because the paper is not about classifiers. Baselines: the compared procedures are the statistical defaults a practitioner would actually use, not a strawman, and Table I honestly scopes what the three closest frameworks do and do not demonstrate. Compute: no GPU, and the entire artifact is public, which is the strongest reproducibility position in this batch. Contamination: low — predictions are subject-out-of-fold, windows never cross session boundaries, and the input checks reject duplicate keys, missing predictions, non-monotone timestamps and subjects assigned to multiple test folds. Breadth: two datasets, one sensor each, two classifier families, one window-length pair. **The single most likely way this claim is wrong: the headline is a Monte Carlo proportion being read as a constant.** 16.9% and 7.2% are two draws from a distribution; across the paper's two seed sets and AXV's third, the HAC arm at 75% overlap spans 6.6%–8.2% and the i.i.d. arm spans 15.0%–18.0%, so the *gap* is somewhere in 8–12 points, not a fixed 9.7. A decision budgeted on "7.2%" is budgeted on a number with roughly ±1 pp of seed noise and unknown dependence on the DGP. Second-most-likely: the real-data factors are a property of these two datasets' label persistence and class balance rather than of overlap, and AXV's recheck shows the dependence correction is already a near-wash at 0% overlap (HAC/i.i.d. 0.84–1.10), so almost all of the measured inflation lives at 75% and would not transfer to a different stride schedule.

**Confidence.** medium — the simulation claim reproduces on independent seeds using the authors' own frozen code, and the arithmetic of the real-data table is exact, but it is one AR(1) generator, one window length, and a real-data half that is observational on two wearable-HAR datasets. Unknowns: whether 1.75–1.94 generalises past phone-accelerometer and thigh-accelerometer HAR; whether the residual ~7% is irreducible or a bandwidth artefact the authors could not rule out; whether the corrected interval is the right choice for any estimand other than the conditional fixed-record one; whether the target-misalignment finding survives a subject count above the 24 used in simulation; the reduction terms, since the repository carries both a `LICENSE` and a `LICENSE_SCOPE.md` and AXV should check the scope before quoting the artifact.

## The question AXV-8 asked

> when two arms are compared over overlapping evaluation windows or overlapping seeds rather than independent records, is the `delta` we write into `experiment_runs` a real effect or an artifact of the shared observations?

**Answer: it depends which of two different mistakes you are making, and AXV is currently making the first one.**

1. **AXV's seeds are not windows.** AXV's three seeds per arm are genuinely independent replications of the whole run, not overlapping crops of one trace. The dependence this paper documents does not apply to AXV's `experiment_runs.delta` as currently constructed. The three seeds are the right unit and AXV should keep them.
2. **But AXV's aggregation rule is undeclared, and that is the live exposure.** The paper's Figure 3(b) is the transferable result: i.i.d. over the wrong unit gives 72.4% Type-I error where the matched unit gives 5.5%. AXV pools per-seed and per-run values into one `delta` with no statement of what the population is, and its `n` is not written down anywhere in `experiment_runs` (the schema has no `n` column, which is a defect this memo should hand to whoever owns the schema). A paper's headline metric and AXV's headline metric are not the same quantity, and the paper's own §V-C shows two defensible metrics on the same frozen predictions disagreeing about which model won.

**The aggregation rule AXV would have to adopt**, in the paper's terms (§III-B, §VI): declare the *target population* before looking at the metric, then weight by it.

| if AXV's claim is about | highest independent unit | rule | AXV must report |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| these exact 3 seeds on this harness | seed | equal weight per seed | per-seed values, not just the mean |
| the harness in general | run (a seed × a time budget) | equal weight per run, and never pool a short run with a long one | the run as the unit, and the budget it had |
| a practitioner on a different machine | deployment | equal weight per deployment; seeds become a nuisance parameter | the seed spread as a variance, never as evidence of n |

**What it costs AXV in runs it can no longer pool.** Under the third row, the seed is no longer an inference unit, so three seeds do not buy three observations — they buy one observation with a variance estimate. Practically: AXV must stop comparing a 3-seed arm against a 1-seed arm, stop reporting a single-seed delta as a result (it already forbids this in §7.8), and record the time budget alongside every `delta`, because a run that hit the budget and a run that finished are different measurements of the same arm. The concrete change is small — three columns and a pre-registration line — and it is the cheapest correctness fix available to AXV.
