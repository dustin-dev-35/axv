# Reading memo — 2609.31381v1 — Completed Pairs Hide Capped Failures

**Paper:** Completed Pairs Hide Capped Failures: A ReVerPi Case Study of Selective Context Projection - Guangzhe Zhang, [arXiv:2609.31381](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31381v1)

**Authors:** Guangzhe Zhang (1, sole author)

**Claim.** In ReVerPi's recorded 86-run source-reading campaign, a runner that suppresses the
companion arm whenever the first arm fails to complete — Pr(M_2=1 | C_1=0, A_1, X)=0 (Sec 3.3,
Eq. 2) — makes the standard completed-pairs view report an exact tie, 12/15 successes per arm,
which the full 27-run frame contradicts: the sharp finite-frame bounds are Δ_27 ∈ [−9/27, +1/27]
= [−33.3%, +3.7%], and the frozen selector's 3-vs-3 failure tie is carried by one of its own four
fitting cases, outside which it fails 3 to 2 and spends 8.6% more logical tokens (Sec 5.2–5.3,
Tables 3 and 4). ([arXiv:2609.31381](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31381))

**What advanced.** The baseline is not a weaker system, it is *the standard protocol*: analyse
only pairs where both arms completed, on the identical 86 runs and at zero additional compute.
The delta is the width of the identification interval that protocol hides. Completed-pairs-only
gives Δ = 0.0000 exactly (12/15 vs 12/15, Table 7, fifteen completed pairs of eleven jointly
correct, two jointly incorrect, one full-only correct, one projected-only correct). Restoring
all 27 realised boundaries gives Δ_27 ∈ [−9/27, +1/27] (Table 3 + Eq. 5), i.e. projected-minus-
full in [−9, +1] *tasks* out of 27 — a 37.0 percentage-point interval from a reported point
estimate of zero. The cause is 10 unexecuted companions in 10 of 12 stopped pairs, all ten
first-arm caps. Absolute, not relative: 10 of 54 arm-slots missing, c = 10/27 = 0.3704.

The second advance is on the selector, and it is the part AXV cares about more. On the 17 runs
where both bounded outcomes are known, the frozen rule and always-full tie at 3 failures and
+0.9% logical tokens (Table 4, "All comparable": 1,840,140 vs 1,823,900). Stratify out the four
fitting pairs and the tie inverts: 13 runs, selector 3 failures vs full 2, and 1,541,879 vs
1,419,197 logical tokens, **+122,682 tokens, +8.6%** (Table 4, "Outside fitting"). One run,
pathspec-util, is the entire margin: 94,493 history bytes, so the 93,641-byte threshold projects
it; full answers correctly in 3 suffix requests and 84,202 suffix tokens, projected spends 12
requests and 237,629 tokens and returns no answer (Sec 5.5).

Third, on the one fully identified stratum, projection is cheaper in aggregate and more expensive
per pair. On J = 11 jointly correct pairs, logical tokens are 1,267,036 full vs 949,774 projected —
a sum ratio of 0.750, i.e. **25.0% less in aggregate** (Table 5) — while the geometric mean ratio is
0.883, the **median is 1.292 (+29.2%)**, the unweighted mean of ratios is 1.113 (Appendix C), and
suffix requests go 35 → 55 (+57%), with projected worse in 8 of 11 pairs, better in 2, equal in 1
(Sec 5.4). The sign test on 7 of 11 token increases is p = 0.55.

Compute: 86 submitted runs, 641 completed model requests, 7,971,276 input + 231,556 output =
8,202,832 tokens, 74.9% of input reported cached (Sec 5.1). **Zero GPU hours.** Model
`deepseek-flash`, proxy-reported and not authenticated. Protocol: capture pauses before the first
eligible dispatch and seals the tape; both arms replay it; K = 12 newly dispatched suffix requests
per plan; 3,600 s per-phase wall limit; 3 shared archive calls; 1,024-byte excerpt budget
(Table 1). Dataset: investigator-authored questions over fixed subsets of Python package sources.
One stochastic continuation per arm.

**Mechanism.** The paper's causal account has two links, and they have different evidence.
(1) *The estimator is selected on the outcome.* The controller runs branches sequentially and
breaks when a branch is not completed (Sec 3.3), so companion availability is a function of the
first arm's endpoint. Appendix A.3 gives the identity
`H̄_{D=1} − H̄ = Cov_N(H,D)/D̄` for the completed-case mean, notes the covariance has no fixed sign,
and then exhibits the harmful case directly: pathspec-util has H = 1 and D = 0. Inverse-observation
weighting would divide by zero in that stratum and regression would extrapolate into a region of
zero observation probability, so positivity fails and no adjustment recovers the missing
companion. Evidence: **demonstrated**. This is a constructed counterexample plus a complete
enumeration of a 27-row disclosed table, not an estimate, and there is no plausible rival cause for
"12/15 against 12/15 with ten companions never executed". (2) *Projection is specifically harmful.*
Evidence: **asserted**, and it is the weakest load-bearing link. The single adverse case's harm is
mechanical and confounded: the projected arm retrieved archive text successfully, then hit a
shared-call quota refusal whose payload was recorded with `isError=false`, because the recovery
tool returned `isError: true` in its result object while the pinned Pi loop flags a tool failure
only when `execute` throws — an extension/runtime mismatch, fixed after the campaign in PR 15
(Sec 5.5). The paper is careful not to claim the bug caused the cap, and neither do I; but the
paper supplies no second adverse instance, so the sign of the harm is one case deep.

**Alternative routes.**

1. **Run the 10 missing companions and report a point estimate.** — mechanism: the tooling exists
and the quota bug that broke pathspec-util was fixed in PR 15, so a re-run would record both arms
of all 27 boundaries and collapse a 37 pp identification interval to a lattice point on 1/27.
— pros: converts a bound into a measurement, which is what a reader actually wants; the interval
would shrink by exactly the unresolved mass; the pair design and the questions are preserved, so
it is a like-for-like completion of the same experiment. — cons: the tasks were adaptively authored
during the campaign, so even with both arms the result stays in-sample and is not a held-out
estimate; history size is a property of the captured interaction, not of the task (Sec 5.6: six
task identities changed decision between paired and single-arm runs, e.g. more-itertools-recipes
56,382 → 110,194 bytes), so a re-captured boundary at a different size is a different estimand; and
it costs 10 more paired continuations of someone else's harness on someone else's model.
— why-not-chosen: *inference, the paper does not say.* Plausibly a scope decision — the declared
object is the measurement protocol, and Sec 6 is written as advice about future evaluation, not as
a proposal to re-run this campaign. — cost-to-test: 10 paired continuations, ~20 more model
requests per pair, an authenticated `deepseek-flash` account and a ReVerPi build. Not AXV's
$3.25 GPU budget and not an `autoresearch` harness change. — **would-it-win: maybe.** It wins on
the number and loses the contribution: the artefact the paper analyses would no longer exist.

2. **Report only the Eq. 5 interval and the J = 11 stratum; drop the selector entirely.**
— mechanism: Table 3, Eq. 5 and J = 11 are the only parts of the paper that involve no filter at
all, so the paper could consist of those plus the runner reconstruction, and every filtered
estimate disappears. — pros: no filter survives anywhere in the paper; J = 11 is fully identified
(every one of the 12 incomplete pairs has at least one observed zero, so no missing outcome moves
membership) so the one cost contrast left is safe unconditionally; the paper becomes a clean
demonstration of the defect with no residual selection to defend. — cons: it discards the paper's
most transferable result, that a fitting case can carry an aggregate tie, which is the lesson a
reader can act on; and it loses pathspec-util, the one concrete worked instance of the failure
mode, which is most of the paper's teaching value. — why-not-chosen: *inference.* Plausibly
because a methodological case study needs a live worked example, and because the fitting/
outside-fitting split is the paper's own best demonstration of the lesson it is arguing for.
— cost-to-test: zero; it is a subtraction. — **would-it-win: maybe.** Strictly more conservative,
and it removes one remaining filtered estimate (the 17-run "All comparable" row of Table 4, whose
membership is itself selected: 15 completed pairs plus the 2 full-complete/projected-cap pairs),
at the cost of the transferable finding.

3. **Abandon the paired-continuation contract; run every arm independently under a fixed
allowance and compare distributions.** — mechanism: pass@k over k independent samples per task
makes capping part of the outcome rather than a missing observation, so there is no companion to
suppress and the entire class of defect disappears. — pros: it removes the failure mode instead of
patching it; it needs no partial-identification machinery at all; it is already AXV's own
protocol, three independent seeds per arm, each run terminal. — cons: it is far more expensive —
the paper's shared prefix totals 510,392 tokens across 17 runs (Table 11), so replay is doing real
work, and 74.9% of the campaign's input tokens were cache hits; independent runs confound arm
with prefix, which is precisely what the sealed-tape design exists to prevent; and the paper's
argument is that matched continuation is the *right* contract, so abandoning it is a larger claim
than a 27-run case study can support. — why-not-chosen: *inference.* Plausibly because the paired
contract is the object under study and because the prefix replay is what makes the frame affordable
at all. — cost-to-test: ~k× the requests; at k=3 that is 2–3× the campaign, ~1,300 requests.
— **would-it-win: no, for this paper. Yes, for a protocol that has not already invested in a
paired design** — which is the actual recommendation for anyone starting fresh.

4. **Remove the selector: report the 27-run bound and J = 11, and treat the threshold as an
artefact.** — mechanism: the selector is one rule, `r = 1{h ≥ 93,641}`, fitted on four completed
pairs; the threshold equals the fitting history size of tomlkit-datetimes (Sec 3.4), and the
hindsight optimum 95,519 equals the fitting history size of packaging-compatibility. Both
cutpoints are literally fitted values. — pros: deletes the only part of the paper whose number is
a function of four adaptively chosen cases; removes the strongest-looking 8.6% claim, whose own
leave-one-source-out version is *worse* than the frozen rule (12 of 14 folds pick 95,519, but the
concatenated held-out choices incur 4 failures against the frozen rule's 3); the surviving paper is
narrower and much harder to attack. — cons: it deletes the concrete illustration of
fitting-versus-evaluation separation, which is the section practitioners will reuse; and it
removes the only place the paper shows a *positive* result (the genuine large savings), so the
remainder reads as purely destructive. — why-not-chosen: *inference.* Plausibly because the
author wants to show the fitting artefact from both sides, including where it flattered the rule.
— cost-to-test: zero. — **would-it-win: maybe.** A more robust paper, and a less useful one.

**Evidence quality.**

| Dimension | Assessment |
| --- | --- |
| Seeds | **One stochastic continuation per arm, everywhere.** The paper states it (Sec 6, "Limits and reproducibility"). No per-pair repeat exists, so every success difference in the paper is a single draw on an adaptive task. This is the largest weakness. |

What is absent: any ablation of the runner's stop rule itself, which is the thing the paper says matters. |
| Baselines | Always-full is the obvious control and is reported at equal effort (Table 11: 3 failures, 1,823,900 logical tokens). Weak in one specific way: every other policy — always-projected, frozen threshold, hindsight oracle — lives inside the same single-feature history-threshold family, so the paper cannot say whether history bytes is the right feature. No competitor system is compared. |

| **Most likely way the claim is wrong** | The claim as scoped in the abstract is arithmetic and cannot be wrong. The claim a reader will *carry away* — that selective context projection is harmful — rests on one case whose harm is confounded with an unrelated tooling bug: pathspec-util's projected arm retrieved archive text correctly and then hit a shared-call quota refusal that the pinned Pi loop did not surface as an error, because the extension returned `isError: true` in its result object while the loop flags failure only on a thrown `execute` (Sec 5.5, fixed in PR 15). **Confidence.** `high` — the claim as scoped is a finite sum over 27 disclosed integers with no
sampling step, and I reproduced Table 3, the 12/15 tie and the Eq. 5 bounds to 1e-12 from the
paper's own Tables 7 and 8. The multi-seed criterion in the checklist does not bind on an identity,
and I say so rather than quietly claiming `high` by default; the *directional* sub-claim, that
projection is worse, is `low` and this memo does not rest on it. Unknowns: whether the
projected-minus-full contrast is actually non-positive once pathspec-util is re-run with the error
flag fixed; whether the 12 stopped pairs would mostly have succeeded as projected, which is the
only thing that would move the [−9,+1] bound; whether the 37.0 pp width is representative of other
campaigns or is specific to a 12-request suffix cap; whether history bytes is the right selector
feature at all, since the paper only searches inside that one family; and whether my
re-derivation from the paper's printed tables agrees with the authors' own `anc/analysis/derive.py`,
which I did not execute.

  **Caveat:** the study verifies the estimator, not an agent. It inherits the paper's assumption
  that an unresolved companion is bounded by [0, 1] with no cross-run restriction, which is
  exactly what makes the width equal the unresolved fraction; any monotone structure across runs
  would tighten the bounds, and this paper does not supply it.

  **The answer to AXV's two questions.** *Smallest change:* at batch allocation time, reserve each
  arm its own wall-clock slice up front and forbid one arm's overrun from consuming another's, so
  Pr(M_2 = 1 | C_1 = 0, A_1, X) goes from 0 to 1. Necessary complement, one line: every allocated
  run id gets a terminal leaderboard record with `metric_value: null`, `censored: true`,
  `censor_reason ∈ {crash, pod-lost, oom, timeout}`, `decision: inconclusive`. That is not
  hypothetical — I read it in our own wrapper. `run-experiment.sh:116` raises `SystemExit` when no
  `val_bpb` is found, and the ledger write is the `record` call at line 148, *after* it, so an
  allocated arm that gets censored leaves no row at all and any later contrast over surviving rows
  is completed-pairs-only. That is AXV's own 12/15 tie, present in our tooling today. Cost: $0.00.
  No GPU, no pod, no extra wall clock; it touches the batch manifest and the wrapper's exit path,
  not `train.py`, so `config_fingerprint` and the comparability cohort are unchanged and every
  existing ledger record stays valid. *Smallest n:* **no n does, and that is the point.** AXV's
  standing 3-seed rule makes a 2-arm contrast n = 6, which is safe only while c < 1/6 ≈ 0.167; at
  c = 0.20, three of every four such batches are censored, and at the paper's c = 0.37 the largest
  honest paired batch is n = 2. Worse, c cannot be measured today, because a censored arm writes
  no row — so c_hat is biased toward zero by exactly the defect being measured, and the pre-check
  that would tell you your batch is too large is unavailable. The fix is therefore a *labelling*
  change, not a sizing change: once every allocated run id has a terminal row, the distinction
  between "no difference" and "we stopped looking" is available at any n, because the report
  carries an interval instead of a point estimate. n then buys lattice precision and never width
  (a01: 37.0 pp flat from N = 27 to N = 2700).
