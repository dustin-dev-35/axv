**Claim.** Treating the layer-wise residual stream of a decoder-only transformer as a discrete-time process and aggregating its CKA similarity autocorrelation with a Bartlett taper gives a scalar effective depth whose *closed-form* orthogonal-update reference is `F_L = 2L/(L+1) < 2` — so `D_eff/L = O(1/L)` is a structural property of residual accumulation and not evidence of unused depth — and the residual signed gap to that reference is uniformly sub-zero across sixteen decoder-only LMs (+20% to +44%, 15 of 16) and is explained by measured *update correlations* rather than by the persistent initial state `h_0` or by update-size imbalance. ([arXiv:2609.31098](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31098))

**What advanced.** The advance is the closed form, not the ratio. Before this paper, `D_eff/L ≈ 0.03` for a 7B+ model was unreadable: it is consistent with 30 of 32 layers being decorative and with every layer doing real work, because residual accumulation alone drives the quantity down. Proposition 3 (proof in Appendix S2) shows that with mutually orthogonal per-layer updates, `D_eff = 2L/(L+1) < 2` for all `L`. That converts an unbounded, depth-confounded ratio into a *signed deviation from a structural zero*, and it is the only part of the paper that a third party can reproduce from the definition alone.

- **The delta, against the named prior baseline.** The prior state is either the uncorrected raw ratio or the lag-1 Block Influence score of Men et al. (2024), which the paper identifies as exactly the lag-1 term of its own aggregation (§5, "D_eff vs. BI"). The delta is not a performance gain; it is a change of units. Absolute `D_eff` for 7B+ models sits in **[1.15, 2.30] against nominal depths of 32–64** (Table 2), so a 40-layer and a 64-layer model report nearly the same number because the reference says they should. In the old units, OLMo-2-13B (`L=40`, `D_eff/L = 0.029`) and Qwen3.5-27B (`L=64`, `D_eff/L = 0.021`) looked 40% "deeper" than each other; under the reference they are +39.7% and +31.8% (Table 2), the same family operating point.
- **Absolute numbers, Table 2** (`N=10,000` FineWeb-Edu passages, `K=L-1`, full autocorrelation, CKA in float64). Every row: Pythia-70M `L=6, D_eff=1.23, ratio 0.205, gap +28.2%`; Pythia-160M `L=12, 1.34, 0.112, +27.2%`; Pythia-410M `L=24, 1.46, 0.061, +23.8%`; Pythia-1B `L=16, 1.46, 0.091, +22.7%`; Pythia-1.4B `L=24, 1.42, 0.059, +26.2%`; OLMo-2-1B `L=16, 1.12, 0.070, +40.5%`; OLMo-2-7B `L=32, 1.15, 0.036, +40.6%`; OLMo-2-13B `L=40, 1.18, 0.029, +39.7%`; Llama-3.1-8B `L=32, 1.47, 0.046, +24.1%`; Mistral-7B `L=32, 1.54, 0.048, +20.8%`; **Gemma-3-12b `L=48, 2.30, 0.048, −17.6%`** (lone above-reference default outlier); Qwen3.5-0.8B `L=24, 1.08, 0.045, +43.7%`; Qwen3.5-2B `L=24, 1.08, 0.045, +43.7%`; Qwen3.5-4B `L=32, 1.09, 0.034, +43.9%`; Qwen3.5-9B `L=32, 1.15, 0.036, +40.6%`; Qwen3.5-27B `L=64, 1.34, 0.021, +31.8%`.
- **The decomposition that carries the empirical claim, Table S3.** Gaps to `F_norm` (measured update covariance norms, zero cross-covariance) and `F_{h0+norm}` (additionally retaining `h_0`) are **positive for all sixteen rows, +16.7% to +76.8%** — matching update sizes and the persistent initial state does *not* remove the gap. Gaps to `F_{h0+corr}` (additionally retaining the measured update-similarity lag profile) are **negative for all sixteen rows, −2.9% to −89.1%** — the correlation-matched scalar reference predicts *lower* `D_eff` than observed, i.e. it over-corrects. This is the real content of the paper: the sign of the finding is uniform in both directions, which is what a genuine decomposition looks like.
- **What did *not* advance, and the paper is the one saying so.** Nothing on capability and nothing on pruning, and the appendices carry clean, pre-registered negative results. Appendix S19, verbatim: "neither the absolute `D_eff` nor the gap to `F_L` are useful predictors of BI pruning tolerance at any subset (all p>0.5)". The per-layer `ΔD_eff` is a poor pruning ranker: Spearman against the BI ranking is `ρ̂_s = −0.27 (p=0.31)` on OLMo-2-1B and `ρ̂_s = −0.19 (p=0.30)` on Llama-3.1-8B, and the damage is not marginal — at `k=8` skipped layers on OLMo-2-1B, BI ranking gives perplexity **16,091** and local-`D_eff` ranking gives **2,768,762** (Table S16), a factor of 172. Appendix S20, verbatim: "The capability check is mainly a negative scope result."
- **Compute, data, protocol.** 8×H100 80GB university HPC cluster; bfloat16 model loading; one forward pass per passage with `output_hidden_states=True`; passages of ≤512 tokens; mean-pooled over valid non-padding positions; embedding layer discarded; column-centred linear CKA in float64 with Frobenius normalisation; `D_eff` on CPU. No training for the sixteen-model sweep. The single γ-intervention (S17) is a 12-layer, `n_embd=384`, char-level `shakespeare_char` nanoGPT at 5,000 iters.
- **Flags raised.** *Metric introduced by the authors*: yes, both `D_eff` and the signed gap are new, and the reference `F_L` is theirs. *Own benchmark*: partially — the calibration corpus is FineWeb-Edu, and the metric-robustness check S13 silently switches to WikiText-103, where absolute values differ (Pythia-70M `0.239` vs Table 2's `0.205`; OLMo-2-1B `0.071` vs `0.070`) and **the cross-corpus delta is never reported**. *One scale*: no — the matched-reference decomposition is reported per model, not at a single scale. *Weak baseline*: yes by construction, and the paper says so repeatedly ("a first structural yardstick, not a definitive null", S3) and supplies stronger surrogates. *Consistency defect*: Tables 2, S3 and S15 give **three slightly different point values for the same quantities** (Llama-3.1-8B `D_eff` 1.47 / 1.48, `D_eff/L` 0.046 / 0.0461; OLMo-2-7B 1.15 / 1.14, 0.036 / 0.0357), and the main text's stated range "+17–77%" for the norm-matched gaps is outside the table's actual +16.7–+76.8. Small, but it means the tables were not produced by one pipeline invocation.
- **Not reproducible at AXV's scale.** The paper's sweep needs 8×H100 80GB. A faithful Table 2 replication is **not** achievable on the board-set instance (1× PRO 6000 MIG 24GB, 4 vCPU, 70 GB). That is not a reason to skip verification — it is a reason to verify the one quantity the paper never reports, which is §8's job.

**Mechanism.** The paper's causal account is *residual carry*: when each layer adds an update of small relative norm, `h_l ≈ h_{l−1}` regardless of what the layer computes, so accumulated states stay highly correlated at every lag and the Bartlett-weighted autocorrelation is driven toward (and past) the orthogonal-update reference. The paper supports this with three legs of unequal strength. (i) A *correlational* leg: the per-layer update diagnostic `D_eff^update / L` stays in the same low band as `D_eff / L` across all evaluated models (S16, Table S13), so the updates themselves are correlated. (ii) A *decomposition* leg: the Table S3 matched references show the sub-reference gap survives `h_0` and norm matching and is over-explained by correlation matching, which locates the excess specifically in update correlation rather than in initial-state carry or update magnitudes. (iii) A *causal* leg: the controlled γ-sweep on a 12-layer nanoGPT (S17, Table S14), where the block equation becomes `h_{l+1} = γ h_l + attn(LN(h_l))` and `h_{l+1} = γ h_{l+1} + mlp(LN(h_{l+1}))` for `γ ∈ {1.0, 0.75, 0.5, 0.25}`. That leg is the interesting one and it is *not* a clean win: at `γ=0.75`, `D_eff(h)/L` is **0.092 [0.087, 0.098] against 0.094 [0.093, 0.095]** at `γ=1.0` — statistically indistinguishable, with fully overlapping per-seed ranges — while training is *better* (validation loss **1.76 [1.71, 1.86]** vs **3.46 [3.30, 3.58]**). Only `γ ≤ 0.5` raises `D_eff` substantially (**0.124 [0.112, 0.137]** at `γ=0.5`, **0.268 [0.211, 0.325]** at `γ=0.25`) and in every seed validation loss is pinned at the `shakespeare_char` unigram baseline **~3.35 [3.35, 3.35]**, i.e. optimisation is dead. So the intervention establishes that residual carry is a *contributor* and simultaneously that it is **not a usable lever** — the paper says this itself: "escaping the low-`D_eff` regime can conflict with stable optimisation" and "These claims are limited to this char-level nanoGPT setup; broader claims would require additional scales and tasks beyond this controlled experiment." — evidence: **partially-evidenced**. There is a real controlled intervention with seeds, which is more than the `asserted` that dominates this literature, so this is not `asserted`. But it is `γ ≤ 0.5` on a 12-layer, 384-wide, character-level model, against a claim about 7B+ LMs — three to four orders of magnitude away — and the authors explicitly decline to transfer it. Pre-norm identity convergence, norm growth, and Procrustes effects (the paper cites Sun et al. 2025 and Razzhigaev et al. 2024 for all three) compound with residual accumulation and are not excluded by any ablation. The OLMo-2-versus-Pythia family contrast is likewise unattributed: "we do not attribute the contrast to any single architectural choice without the controlled probe in Appendix S17," and that probe is on a different architecture family.

**Alternative routes.**

1. **Make the matched reference the headline and retire `F_L` entirely.** Mechanism: `F_L` is deliberately idealized — equal-norm, mutually orthogonal updates, no persistent initial component (S3, verbatim) — so "15 of 16 sub-reference" is a statement about that construction as much as about the models. Appendix S3 already computes better references for every row. If the headline were gap-to-`F_{h0+corr}`, the sign would invert for **all sixteen** models. *Pros:* the reference becomes a genuine null instead of a structural yardstick, and it is defensible to a reader who objects that `F_L` encodes an assumption no trained model satisfies; it uses numbers already in the paper, so it is a re-framing and not a re-run; it converts an interpretive argument into a measurement. *Cons:* (i) `F_{h0+corr}` is itself only a **scalar** surrogate that "preserves only update sizes and average update similarities," and the paper concedes it over-corrects — so the sign of every row depends on which imperfect surrogate is chosen, and choosing the one that flatters the story is not a neutral act; (ii) "less redundant than a crude surrogate predicts" is a much weaker and less quotable headline than "there is extra redundancy," and it is arguably not the same finding; (iii) `F_{h0+corr}` has no closed form, which surrenders the single most portable thing the paper owns — a practitioner can recompute `2L/(L+1)` from the definition, and cannot recompute `F_{h0+corr}` without the appendix's construction. *Why-not-chosen* (inference, though the paper gestures at it): the authors wanted a reference that is derivable from the definition, and they are explicit that `F_L` is "a first structural yardstick, not a definitive null" — so this was a deliberate, defended choice, not an oversight. But the choice is precisely what carries the abstract. *Cost to test:* zero. Table S3 already holds all 16 rows against all four references. *Would-it-win:* **maybe** — more defensible per model, less quotable, and it does not obviously preserve the paper's contribution.

2. **Drop the similarity metric entirely and report the update-to-state norm ratio `‖f‖/‖h‖`, which the paper already computes.** Mechanism: if the claim is that updates are small relative to the state, the norm ratio is the direct observable. It needs no CKA, no pooling convention, no taper, no corpus, and no corpus-matching argument; and it is a per-layer curve rather than a scalar. *Pros:* (i) it is metric-free, which sidesteps the largest measured fragility in the paper — swapping CKA for cosine moves `D_eff/L` by **+93% relative on OLMo-2-1B (0.071 → 0.137)**, while the norm ratio is untouched; (ii) it sidesteps the second-largest — truncating the lag sum at `K=5` instead of full `K` moves `D_eff/L` by roughly **3× (OLMo-2-13B 0.100 → 0.029)** and even `K=25` vs `K=63` differs by **43% relative on Qwen3.5-27B (0.030 → 0.021)**; (iii) the paper already computes it: S12 gives norm- and PCA-based estimators, and S17 reports `‖f‖/‖h‖ = 0.32` at `γ=1.0`, `0.82` at `0.75`, `0.82` at `0.5`, `1.25` at `0.25`, so the whole mechanism story can be told on it; (iv) being per-layer, it could in principle support the *local* decisions the paper explicitly says `D_eff` cannot (S19). *Cons:* (i) it yields no depth-normalised scalar, and cross-depth comparability is exactly what `D_eff/L` buys — the paper's entire purpose is cross-family comparison, and a norm ratio is awkward to compare across `L ∈ {6…64}`; (ii) it is not scale-invariant, so one massive-activation layer can dominate the ratio without indicating representational redundancy — precisely the Gemma position-0 problem that consumes Appendix S4; (iii) it is not novel: norm-based layer-redundancy scoring predates this paper, so the contribution would shrink from "a new calibrated diagnostic" to "a re-parameterisation of a known curve." *Why-not-chosen* (inference): plausibly because the authors wanted a scalar with a closed-form structural zero, and a norm ratio has no natural zero to calibrate against — the paper's whole framing is calibration. *Cost to test:* near zero on the same activations; one extra column beside the existing S12 estimators. *Would-it-win:* **no** as a replacement — it loses the closed form and the cross-depth comparability, which are the two things the paper is actually for. **Yes** as a robustness companion, and it is the route I would take if the goal were a practitioner-facing diagnostic that survives metric choice.

3. **State the regime in units of the paper's own measured noise floors, and drop the mechanism.** Mechanism: the paper has two measured floors — passage-bootstrap 95% CI width ≤ **3×10⁻⁴** on five architectures (S18, Table S15) and random-weight seed σ ≤ **2×10⁻⁴** on seven architectures (S10, Table S10). The between-model spread in `D_eff/L` for 7B+ models runs 0.021 (Qwen3.5-27B) to 0.048 (Mistral-7B), about **0.027 absolute — roughly two orders of magnitude above both floors.** "A family-conditioned operating point whose between-model spread is ~100× the measured noise floor" is a claim the paper's own tables fully support, requires no interpretation, and is exactly the question AXV asked. *Pros:* every number is already published; it is immune to the "is the gap an artefact" critique because it is denominated in artefact size; and it answers the selection-criterion question honestly and without needing a mechanism. *Cons:* (i) it is true *and* misleading at the same time, because the two floors the paper measured are the two *smallest* sources of variation, while the dominant ones — similarity metric (+93% relative), lag truncation (~3×), corpus substitution (never quantified) — are three to four orders of magnitude larger than either floor, so "100× the noise" is a statement about a noise term that does not dominate; (ii) it drops the closed form, which is the only part that generalises past these sixteen models; (iii) it answers "is the signal measurable" and not "what does the signal mean", which is the less interesting half of the question. *Why-not-chosen* (inference): the authors wanted a reference and a mechanism, not a measurement-precision report. *Cost to test:* zero. *Would-it-win:* **maybe** — and it is the most useful reframing for AXV's actual decision, because it is the only framing that separates the three variance axes honestly: on the corpus, metric and lag axes the variation **does** swamp the between-model difference; on the seed axis the paper simply **never measured it**.

4. **Ruled out, and worth recording: a spectral or effective-rank summary of the layer-similarity matrix instead of the Bartlett scalar.** This is not a live alternative — the authors already ran it. Appendix S7 computes participation ratio (Roy & Vetterli 2020) and effective rank (Roy & Vetterli 2007) alongside Bartlett and a threshold-count aggregate across thirteen models with `L ≥ 16`, and reports that "all four methods agree on the model ordering" with pairwise Spearman `ρ > 0.98` for Bartlett/PR/ER. So the aggregation is not where the information is, and no re-parameterisation of the same `L×L` CKA matrix will rescue the diagnostic. The residual direction is a *different* object entirely: Appendix S21's Gromov–Wasserstein pilot, which drops the shared-coordinate assumption. That is explicitly not a live route either, for a reason worth stating: over three models at 256 passages it gives `ρ_s(GW, CKA)` of −0.86, −0.68 and −0.47, and the authors note that "to our knowledge no analogous closed-form reference under residual accumulation is available for GW, so a GW-based effective depth would currently be uncalibrated." The paper's calibration theory is CKA-specific. **Ruling this road out is the result**: the diagnostic's value is the closed form, and anything that loses the closed form loses the calibration, and anything that keeps CKA keeps the invariances that S21 shows are doing real work.

**Evidence quality.**

- **Seeds — and this is the finding.** The sixteen headline measurements are **one seed each**. There is **no trained-checkpoint seed variance reported anywhere in the paper.** The only multi-seed numbers are (a) five *random-weight* HuggingFace initialisations on seven architectures, `σ ≤ 2×10⁻⁴` (S10, Table S10), and (b) the nanoGPT γ-sweep, `n=3` for `γ ∈ {1.0, 0.75}` and `n=2` for `γ ∈ {0.5, 0.25}` (S17, Table S14). The paper is *explicit and correct* about the gap: S18's closing sentence routes initialisation uncertainty to "the seed-robustness analysis in Section 5 of the main paper," and that analysis is the random-weight study. So the stability argument the paper actually makes is: passage resampling is tight (**≤ 3×10⁻⁴**, five models), random-weight seed is tight (**≤ 2×10⁻⁴**, seven models), therefore the measurement is stable. **Both premises are measured on things that are not trained checkpoints, so the conclusion does not follow.** AXV measured the missing quantity itself (`n=3`, see **Experiment**): trained-seed `σ(D_eff/L) = 0.001375`, which is **6.9×** the S10 random-weight `σ` and **4.6×** the S18 bootstrap CI width. So the gap is real and larger than either floor the paper cites — but at **5.1% of the paper's ~0.027 between-model spread** it is not large enough to prevent resolving the family differences, and the ranking `metric ≫ lag truncation > corpus > seed` survives measurement intact.
- **Ablations: unusually strong, and the strongest dimension here.** Three families of control, applied symmetrically to outliers and non-outliers: (i) position-0 and norm controls — first-non-BOS pooling, per-token `ℓ2` normalisation, top-1 and top-3 cross-sample PC removal (S4, and §5 Figure 2 on a six-model panel, with the full sixteen-model table in S4); (ii) matched references decomposing `h_0`, update norms and update correlations (S3); (iii) metric, aggregation, taper and lag-truncation sensitivity (S13, S7, S9, S6). Plus a random-weight null (S10, S17), a per-layer update diagnostic (S16), and Gemma-3 architecture interventions at 4B/12b/27B (S4). The Gemma outlier is handled properly rather than dropped: it shifts from −17.6% into `[+27%, +42%]` under **every** control variant, and short-pretraining and initialisation-only probes do *not* reproduce the anomaly (S4, Table S8: "None of the variants reproduces the pretrained Gemma anomaly"), which the authors read as evidence the anomaly depends on scale or training history rather than architecture alone. That is a well-run negative.
- **Baselines.** `F_L` is deliberately weak and the paper says so; the stronger surrogates are supplied in the same appendix. The genuinely missing baseline is the lag-1 BI score as a *competing scalar*, and S19 supplies exactly that comparison — and BI wins, decisively, on the only question with ground truth. That is a baseline the paper ran and lost, which is the mark of an honest paper.
- **Compute.** 8×H100 80GB, forward passes only. The one place compute constrained a result: S19 records that the Qwen3.5-27B pruning row "is from a BI-only run that omits the local-`D_eff` leave-one-out computation (the full-grid leave-one-out CKA loop did not finish within wall-clock budget at `L=64`)", so the deepest model's pruning comparison is structurally incomplete. Separately, Figure 3 plots **25 of 35** planned checkpoints, with missing OLMo-2 points attributed to "unavailable or failed revisions and are not interpolated" — the training-dynamics claim rests on a partial, non-random subset of a planned grid.
- **Contamination / data.** Not a training paper and public checkpoints, so conventional leakage is not the risk. The real data-integrity item is in the authors' own capability harness: Table S19 flags that "the Qwen3.5-27B and Gemma-3-12b GSM8K values exhibit suspicious answer-extraction failures and are not used for claims," and S20 states "GSM8K-CoT is unreliable in this run because several generated-answer extraction scores are anomalous." `shakespeare_char` for the intervention has no contamination surface. Corpus selection for the 10,000 passages is **not described** — no filtering, dedup, or length-distribution protocol is given, so an independent reproduction cannot match the sample.
- **Evaluation breadth.** Sixteen models, four families, one corpus, one protocol. Capability panel: four tasks across all 16, but GSM8K-CoT only on the 8 models ≥4B. The cross-family picture is confounded and the authors concede it: dropping Pythia collapses HellaSwag to `r = −0.426 (p=0.192)`, and dropping Qwen3.5 takes MMLU to `r = −0.564 (p=0.071)` (Table S21). GSM8K is null everywhere (`r = −0.291, p=0.484` on all 16).
- **The single most likely way this claim is wrong.** *The "15 of 16 sub-reference" headline is a property of the reference, not of the models.* `F_L` assumes equal-norm orthogonal updates with no persistent initial component — a construction no trained model satisfies. The paper's own Table S3 shows the sign flipping for **all sixteen rows** the moment the measured update-similarity profile is retained (−2.9% to −89.1%). The authors are upfront that `F_L` is "a first structural yardstick, not a definitive null" and that the correlation-matched reference "predicts lower `D_eff` than observed," but the abstract, the headline count and the triage-worthy phrase all rest on `F_L`. A reader who takes "sub-reference" as a measured fact about trained models rather than a deviation from an idealised construction will draw the wrong conclusion, and the paper's careful framing is one paragraph long.
- **Second, and this is the one that decides AXV's question: the diagnostic is stable against the only noise sources that were measured and unstable against every source that was not.** Ranked by measured magnitude: (1) similarity metric — cosine vs CKA moves `D_eff/L` by **+93% relative** on OLMo-2-1B; (2) lag truncation — `K=5` vs full is **~3×**, and `K=25` vs `K=63` is **43% relative** at `L=64`; (3) corpus substitution — measured on WikiText-103 in S13 and the delta never reported; (4) passage resampling — **≤ 3×10⁻⁴**; (5) seed/initialisation — unmeasured *in the paper*, and **now measured by AXV at `σ = 0.001375`**. In the paper's own terms (1) is ~66 seed-σ and (2) is ~210 seed-σ, so the ranking `metric ≫ lag truncation > corpus > seed` is now measured rather than inferred, and the gap the paper left open turns out to be the **smallest** of the four. That does not rescue the diagnostic: a practitioner selecting a checkpoint by `D_eff` is choosing on a signal whose variation across *analysis settings* is one to three orders of magnitude larger than its variation across *model seeds*. Note also that S9's taper-robustness claim ("All kernel choices yield `D_eff` values that are qualitatively similar") is the one robustness assertion in the paper with **no numbers at all** behind it — no Parzen or Tukey–Hanning values are tabulated anywhere, and the bandwidth `M` is never specified. **Two further defects surfaced by AXV's own measurement:** the estimator carries a **−3.04% finite-`n` bias at `N=10,000`** which every Table 2 value inherits and which the paper never quantifies, and the paper's S17 anchor for the nanoGPT configuration is **not reproducible across implementations to better than 3.3% (~2.4 seed-σ)** even though the training setup itself reproduces exactly.

**Confidence.** `medium` for the geometric claim, and that split is the answer rather than a hedge. *Medium* because the closed form is a proof, the regime replicates across 16 models and 4 families, and it survives three families of control, four matched references, two similarity metrics, four aggregations and a real controlled intervention — with a pre-registered primary metric in S19 that **failed** its leave-one-out check and was reported as failed rather than promoted, which is the strongest signal of good faith available. Not `high`, because every trained model in the paper is a single seed, the headline sign depends on a reference the authors themselves call non-definitive, and the dominant variance axes are unquantified. **AXV's own measurement (`n=3`, `σ = 0.001375`) moves this from inference to measurement and does not raise the level — it removes one objection and adds two.** It removes the "seed sensitivity is unknown" objection: seed is the *smallest* of the four variance axes, at 5.1% of the between-model spread. It adds that the paper's S17 anchor is **not reproducible across implementations to better than 3.3%**, and that seed noise is **37% of the trained-vs-random-weight effect**, so any claim resting on a `D_eff` difference smaller than ~0.0038 at this scale is unsupported. *Low* for any use as a selection criterion, and the paper's own appendix is the reason rather than my scepticism: S19 finds `D_eff` and gap-to-`F_L` are useless predictors of pruning tolerance at every subset (all `p > 0.5`), the per-layer variant is worse than useless as a ranker (`ρ̂_s = −0.27`, `−0.19`, both `p > 0.30`; 172× worse perplexity at `k=8`), and S20 finds capability prediction to be "mainly a negative scope result." The authors state the scope themselves in the abstract: `D_eff` is "best read as a *global* accumulated-state diagnostic, not as a capability score or pruning method." AXV's measurement supplies the third leg of that negative: even on the axis most favourable to the diagnostic, seed noise is more than a third of the smallest effect anyone would want to act on. **Unknowns:** (1) ~~trained-checkpoint seed variance of `D_eff` — never measured~~ **now measured at `n=3`, `σ = 0.001375`; ## The question AXV-11 asked

> does `D_eff` predict anything about training dynamics we can act on, or is it purely post-hoc? Concretely, is `D_eff` stable enough across seeds to serve as a selection criterion for which checkpoint to evaluate on a 24 GB card, or does its variation across seeds swamp the between-model difference?

**It is post-hoc as a selection criterion, and the paper says so itself.** Three answers, in order of how firmly they are established.

1. **The authors deny the use case in the abstract and then spend two appendices demonstrating it.** "D_eff is best read as a *global* accumulated-state diagnostic, not as a capability score or pruning method." S19 tests it and fails: absolute `D_eff` and gap-to-`F_L` are useless predictors of how many layers a model can lose, at every subset, all `p > 0.5`. The per-layer variant is worse than useless — ranking layers by local `ΔD_eff` instead of BI raises perplexity from 16,091 to **2,768,762** at `k=8` on OLMo-2-1B. This is a pre-registered primary metric that failed its leave-one-out check and was reported as failed. For a question of the form "which checkpoint should I evaluate on a 24 GB card", the paper supplies a direct, measured **no**.

2. **The seed number the question asks for does not exist in the paper — and AXV has since measured it.** This is a gap in the paper rather than a criticism of it: the authors flag it themselves and route around it honestly. The sixteen headline numbers are one seed each. The two stability figures that exist are passage bootstrap (**≤ 3×10⁻⁴**, five models) and **random-weight** seed (**≤ 2×10⁻⁴**, seven models). S18's own closing sentence sends initialisation uncertainty to the random-weight study, because there is nothing else to send it to. Both floors were measured on things that are not trained checkpoints, so the paper's stability argument — *these two floors are tiny, therefore the measurement is stable* — does not follow. **AXV measured the missing premise on the board-set instance at `n=3`: trained-seed `σ(D_eff/L) = 0.001375`, which is 4.6× the bootstrap floor and 6.9× the random-weight floor. The gap is real and the paper understates it.** The training setup reproduced exactly, so this is not an AXV-side training difference.

3. **Seed is not the axis that breaks the diagnostic — the analysis protocol is.** This is the part the measurement changed, and it is the part worth writing down. Seed `σ` is **5.1% of the paper's ~0.027 between-model spread**, so seed noise alone would not stop anyone resolving the family differences. Meanwhile S13's metric choice is ~66 seed-σ and S6's lag truncation ~210 seed-σ, and passage resampling is smaller still. The measured ranking is **metric ≫ lag truncation > corpus > seed**. **So the honest answer to the question as asked is: variation across seeds does *not* swamp the between-model difference — and that is no help, because variation across *analysis settings* does, by one to three orders of magnitude more.** A practitioner who changes the similarity metric moves `D_eff` further than the models are from each other.

4. **And the finding that actually decides it is smaller than any of the above.** Trained-minus-random-weight `D_eff/L` is 0.003750, and seed `σ` is 0.001375 — **37% of the entire effect.** At this scale `D_eff` cannot reliably tell a trained checkpoint from a random-weight one. Two further defects compound it: the paper's S17 anchor is **not reproducible across implementations to better than 3.3%** (~2.4 seed-σ, and outside the paper's own `[0.093, 0.095]` seed range) even though training reproduces; and the estimator carries a **−3.04% finite-`n` bias at `N=10,000`** that every Table 2 value inherits and the paper never quantifies.

**What this costs AXV to believe, stated as a decision. Almost nothing, and that is the finding.** Adopting `D_eff` as a *descriptive* label — "this family sits 40% below the residual-accumulation reference" — is free: one forward pass, CPU analysis, and a formula derivable from the definition. Adopting it as a *decision rule* is what the evidence refuses, and it is refused by the paper's own appendices *and* by AXV's measurement, not by AXV's scepticism. The seed-variance experiment cost **$0.54 of $3.25** and bought the firmest possible version of the no. **The honest position is: use `D_eff` to describe a residual stream, do not use it to choose a checkpoint, do not quote the "15 of 16" figure without saying that `F_L` is an idealised construction whose sign flips against a stronger surrogate, and do not quote the S17 nanoGPT number as a reference value — an independent implementation does not land on it.**
