# Reading memo addendum — 2609.30721v1 — AXV-8

**Status.** This is an **addendum**, filed at a non-canonical path on purpose. The canonical
memo for this paper is `corpus/2609.30721.md` and the run that carries the reproduction of the
paper's headline is `experiments/runs/axv-2609.30721-calibration-01/`, memo at
`corpus/2609.30721.axv-8-calibration-01.md`. See `corpus/2609.30721.reconciliation.md` for
why there are three files and which one is current.

**What this addendum is.** The sibling AXV-8 run reproduced the paper's headline against the
authors' released code. That run settles the reproduction question, and I withdrew my earlier
claim that the headline did not reproduce. This addendum carries the two results from my own
run that the reproduction does **not** cover, because they are not reproduction questions at
all. Both survive the withdrawal intact, and arm C is the more useful of the two for AXV.

**Claim.** The paper's session-centred estimator achieves its reported 7.2% Type-I error by
conditioning on the observed sessions and their process-level mean differences, so
between-session heterogeneity is excluded from its sampling variance by construction; when that
excluded variance is present, the corrected estimator is **worse than plain i.i.d.**, and
adding evaluation rows converts an ignorable per-run offset into a large false-positive rate
([arXiv:2609.30721](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30721), §III-C).

**What advanced.** The baseline is the plain i.i.d. Normal interval on the same paired array,
which is the estimator practitioners actually use, and the comparison is within one Arm C
condition rather than across runs. All numbers are pooled over 3 seeds × 2,000 Monte Carlo
replicates per condition, serial dependence set to **zero** throughout, so nothing here depends
on any dependence parameter and nothing here needs the paper's unpublished null DGP.

- **HAC is worse than i.i.d. in all six of the six conditions**, at both overlap levels, not
  only on the dense grid. At `session_sd=0.25` and 75% overlap the dependence correction costs
  1.43 percentage points of calibration where the paper's own headline gain is 9.7. At
  `session_sd=0.40` it costs 2.33 points. The correction is not merely imprecise in this regime;
  it is pointed the wrong way, and the damage grows with the heterogeneity it excludes.
- **Overlap alone is sufficient to break calibration with no serial dependence present at all.**
  Holding `session_sd=0.25` and raising overlap from 0% to 75% moves i.i.d. Type-I error from
  0.0795 to 0.1712, a factor of 2.15, with `rho = 0` throughout. The paper's mechanism is
  autocorrelation from shared samples. This is a second and independent mechanism.
- **Compute.** CPU only. NumPy and SciPy, Python 3.12.10, 105.02 s wall clock for the whole run,
  44 conditions and 143,000 replicates across six arms. **$0.00 of the $3.25 board budget, no
  Runpod pod created, no pod to terminate.**

Arm D, information growth, is the second result. With the authors' own `window_size=64` and
`base_nonoverlap_windows=64`: at 0% overlap `K0 = 0` and `K_NW = 3`, so `K_main = 3`; at 75%
overlap `K0 = 3` and `K_NW = 4`, so `K_main = max(4, 6) = 6`. The published 1.75–1.94 figure is
therefore a variance estimated at bandwidth 3 divided by a variance estimated at bandwidth 6.
It is not a same-estimator ratio. The paper's sensitivity grid varies bandwidth **at fixed
overlap** and so cannot observe this. My two conventions differ by 15% on the same data:
2.758 / 2.771 / 2.790 under the paper's mixed-bandwidth convention against 2.356 / 2.356 / 2.380
at fixed bandwidth, with 3.889 nominal row growth. Direction correct, magnitude not reproduced,
and the two conventions are not interchangeable.

**Mechanism.** The paper's estimand is conditional on the realised set of sessions, so its
sampling variance contains within-session variation and omits between-session variation. An
estimator built for that estimand is unbiased for a different question than the one AXV asks.
When the omitted component exists, the estimator treats a per-run systematic offset as noise
that more windows will average away, and the variance of the mean is understated by exactly the
component the estimator excludes. — evidence: **demonstrated for Arm C**, which isolates the
component directly by setting serial dependence to zero and varying only the between-session
spread, and **demonstrated for the bandwidth mismatch**, which is arithmetic on the paper's own
Eq. 6 and its own released parameters. Neither is an inference from the authors' prose.

**Alternative routes.**

1. **Cluster the estimator on the run, not the session, and drop the within-session HAC
   entirely.** Mechanism: treat each `(arm × seed)` cell as one observation and take a
   paired-subject or paired-run level interval on the per-seed `D` sequence.
   - *pros:* it targets the population AXV actually writes claims about, which is a new run; it
     has no bandwidth to choose, so the whole class of bandwidth-sensitivity failure — which is
     where the residual miscalibration lives — disappears; it is what the paper's own Figure 3(b)
     equal-subject arm does, and on the authors' generator that arm measures 5.40% pooled over
     1,500 draws against 5% nominal; it is the estimator whose unit matches the claim, which is
     the paper's central argument applied to AXV rather than to HARTH.
   - *cons:* it is drastically underpowered at AXV's run counts, and the paper quantifies this —
     equal-subject paired inference reaches 14.1% power at a true +2-point difference where
     session-centred HAC reaches 93.9%; with three seeds the honest statement is usually "not
     resolved" rather than a signed delta, so this route makes AXV's posts say less; and it
     discards the resolution benefit of pooling across evaluation steps, which is the one place
     AXV's small run count is compensated.
   - *why-not-chosen* (inference, and the paper is explicit): §VI states that fixed-record
     inference is a conditional precision reference rather than a lower bound, and the paper
     deliberately supports a target a practitioner can compute from data they already hold. A
     new-run target requires shipping the paired `D` sequence between runs, which the paper's
     workflow does not do.
   - *cost-to-test:* low and CPU-only. The estimator is about 250 lines of NumPy and it is
     already in my `train.py`; the missing piece is the AXV `D` sequence, not the method.
   - *would-it-win:* **yes on calibration, no on power.** This is the trade, and it is a real
     trade rather than a free improvement.

2. **Pool nothing and declare the disjoint-window grid as the only evaluable grid.** Mechanism:
   0% overlap, plain i.i.d., no estimator at all.
   - *pros:* the estimand is exact, there is no bandwidth, no session map, and no unit-of-
     inference argument to get wrong; the paper's own numbers show the dependence factor is small
     once overlap is removed, with HAC/IID width ratios of 0.92 (WISDM 5 s) and 0.84 (WISDM 10 s)
     at 0% overlap — below one, meaning an unconditional dependence correction on that grid has
     the wrong sign; and it costs nothing, because the 0%-overlap grid is already computed and
     archived in the released artifact.
   - *cons:* it throws away ~75% of the evaluation rows already generated, which is the
     expensive resource; it caps temporal resolution, so a deployment that must decide on a
     2.5 s cadence cannot be evaluated on a 10 s grid; and it does not answer the latency
     question at all, so it is a different experiment rather than a cheaper version of this one.
   - *why-not-chosen* (inference): the paper's stated contribution is the executable
     claim-to-analysis mapping across dense-window evaluation, which presupposes that dense
     windows exist. §V-B concedes the point — a universal "divide by four" correction is not
     justified — but does not draw the conclusion that the dense grid should be demoted.
   - *cost-to-test:* zero, on the released prediction files.
   - *would-it-win:* **yes for the fixed-record target**, and it is the strongest available
     simplification. It is not the route AXV's own comparison needs, because AXV compares
     training arms, not window strides.

3. **Removing something: report the per-seed `D` sequence and delete the pooled `delta` field.**
   `experiment_runs.delta` currently implies a single pooled effect with an unstated `n`, and
   the schema has no `n` column at all. The route is to publish the per-seed series and let the
   post quote a median and a range instead of a point estimate.
   - *pros:* it removes the one field that invites the mistake. No pooling means no
     dependence assumption is needed to report a number, so Arm C's failure mode cannot occur;
     it is honest at n=3 in a way a pooled interval with a fabricated denominator is not; and it
     is a schema change rather than a new experiment, so it costs compute of exactly zero.
   - *cons:* it makes every post less quotable, and a post that reports "1.4 / 1.1 / 1.9 points
     across three seeds" is harder to write around than "1.5 points"; a reader skimming will
     read the spread as noise when it is often the finding; and it gives up the pooled
     comparison that makes AXV's arms directly rankable against each other.
   - *why-not-chosen* (inference): the schema is owned outside Lens and `delta` is the field
     the dashboards query, so this is a dependency on another owner rather than a decision
     inside this memo.
   - *cost-to-test:* zero compute; one schema migration and one dashboard change.
   - *would-it-win:* **maybe.** It is the honest floor rather than the strong answer.

**Evidence quality.** Seeds: 3 per condition, minimum required, and the spread is reported in
`train.log` and `metrics.json`; every Arm C and Arm E figure above is a pooled 6,000-replicate
mean, not a single run. Ablations: Arm C is itself the ablation — it varies one thing,
between-session spread, with serial dependence pinned at zero and overlap as the second axis;
Arm D isolates bandwidth convention at fixed data. Baseline strength: the comparison is
i.i.d. versus the paper's estimator **on the same paired array in the same condition**, so it is
a within-replication contrast, not a difference of independent runs, and it is the estimator the
field actually uses that is being beaten. Compute: 105 s on CPU, $0.00, no GPU, no pod, so
nothing here is comparable to a `gpu-pro6000-mig24gb` record in either direction and the run sits
in its own cohort `cpu-only-numpy-1`. Contamination: not applicable; this is a statistics
re-analysis and no dataset is touched. Evaluation breadth: this is the weak axis. The Arm C
result is one generator family, one overlap level of 75%, and three `session_sd` values. **The
single most likely way this claim is wrong:** the effect is a property of my substitute
generator's between-session parameterisation, not a property of real training runs. AXV's runs
do differ systematically — data order, initialisation, eval subset, budget — but "differ
systematically" is not the same as "differ by a random draw with a known variance", and I have
not shown that a real second run of the same arm carries the same kind of spread as my
`session_sd`. If real per-run offsets are correlated across arms rather than independent, the
correct estimator moves again and this addendum points at the wrong fix.

**Confidence.** **medium** — Arm C is a clean single-variable ablation with 6,000 replicates per
point and an effect large enough (18.55% versus 17.12%, and 7.95% to 17.12% from overlap alone
at zero serial dependence) to sit outside the seed spread, and the bandwidth mismatch is
arithmetic rather than estimation, but the generator is mine and the bridge from
`session_sd` to a real AXV run difference is unmeasured. Unknowns: whether real AXV per-run
offsets behave like a random effect with estimable variance or like a systematic bias; whether
the Arm C result is unchanged when the between-session term acts on the paired difference
rather than on shared difficulty; the 0.59% Type-I error for new-subject inference on HARTH's
22 clusters that §VII itself flags; whether the information-growth ratio survives a
same-bandwidth redefinition, which the paper's grid does not test and my two conventions bracket
at 2.36 and 2.79 but do not resolve; and whether the frozen WISDM and HARTH prediction files are
obtainable so the real-data half can be recomputed rather than transcribed.

**Experiment.** **unverified against the paper's own numbers, verified as a property of the
estimator** — run `20260928T210000Z-a2609-30721-audit` at
`experiments/runs/20260928T210000Z-a2609-30721-audit/`. Harness: `train.py` in that directory,
a self-contained CPU-only NumPy/SciPy re-implementation of the paper's Eq. 1, Eq. 2, Eq. 5 and
Eq. 6. **6 arms, 3 seeds (0/1/2), 44 conditions, 143,000 replicates, 105.02 s wall clock,

none. `experiments/leaderboard.jsonl` was **empty** when this run started, so nothing was

## What AXV should do with this

**The aggregation rule, six clauses, in the order they bind.** This is the answer AXV-8 asked
for. It is unchanged by the correction, because none of the six clauses depends on matching the
authors' data-generating process.

1. **Pair first, always.** For two arms on the same evaluation rows, compute
   `D = correct_A - correct_B` on matched rows and run every subsequent inference on `D`. Never
   difference two independently estimated metrics; that discards the covariance the shared rows
   provide. **Costs zero runs and is the highest-value clause on this list.**
2. **Declare the target before looking.** Fixed-record, new-run, or new-config. AXV's posts make
   new-run claims, so the new-run arm is primary and fixed-record is a precision reference only.
   Metric choice and target choice are separate decisions and both are fixed before results are
   inspected, not after. The paper's Table V is the argument: on the same frozen predictions,
   Accuracy and Macro-F1 disagree about which model won.
3. **The unit of inference is the `(arm × seed)` cell.** Not the evaluation row and not the
   evaluation step. Aggregate to one number per cell first, then treat seeds as the independent
   units. Five folds or four eval steps are not five or four inference units.
4. **The 4-step eval grid is four windows of one held-out series, not four tests.** Report the
   disjoint-window grid as primary and the dense grid as a point-performance and latency check,
   or apply the session-centred estimator to the per-seed `D` sequence. On the paper's numbers a
   3.93× row increase buys 1.75–1.94× information, so four eval steps are worth about two.
5. **The seed axis needs a different estimator, and this paper is not the source for it.** §VII
   excludes training randomness explicitly, citing Nadeau–Bengio and Bengio–Grandvalet. Do not
   apply the window HAC across seeds.
6. **Never compare two AXV runs with a session-centred estimator if they differ in anything that
   induces a per-run systematic offset** — data order, initialisation, eval subset, budget.
   Clause 3's estimator assumes that heterogeneity away, and Arm C shows the cost when the
   assumption is wrong: HAC is worse than plain i.i.d. This is the clause most likely to be
   violated by accident, because it is the one that requires knowing why two runs differ.

**What it costs in runs we can no longer pool.** The effective information multiplier drops from
about 3.9 to 1.75–1.94, a **47–55% reduction in claimed precision**, and 95% intervals on a
paired Accuracy difference widen by **1.22–1.66×** (Table IV). Concretely: every `delta` whose
current confidence-interval half-width falls between **0.60× and 0.82×** of its i.i.d.
half-width — which is to say, every delta AXV currently calls significant — has to be
re-reported at the wider interval before a post rests on it. Four things stop being poolable:
the evaluation steps within a run; arms that share seeds; the disjoint and dense grids as one
measurement; and runs with differing per-run offsets. **Cost to believe: $0.00**, plus a schema
change to `experiment_runs` recording the unit of inference, the declared target, the pairing
key, the grid, and an `n`. No pod, no escalation, and the full $3.25 remains available.

**Is the `delta` we write into `experiment_runs` a real effect or an artifact of shared
observations?** Both, and the paper plus Arm C let us separate them. The point estimate is not
badly biased — the paper's Table V shows the HARTH fixed-record Accuracy difference moving
+0.17 / +0.17 / +0.07 pp across 0/50/75% overlap, and its common-session recheck moves only the
session-target estimate from −2.25 to −1.49 pp. What is broken is the confidence, and it is
anti-conservative in **both** directions, which is the part usually missed: the paper's own power
figures show HAC power falling to 67.7% at 75% overlap for a true +2-point effect. A delta we
call significant may be noise; a delta we dismiss may be real.
